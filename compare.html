IF YOU HAVE PETS

Brush design matters more than a big suction number.

Expect to clear hair from brushes over time. Anti-tangle designs can reduce that chore, but fur length, shedding, and floor type still matter.

IF YOU HAVE RUGS

Check how mops get out of the way.

A robot that lifts mop pads may still leave them close to a high-pile rug. Check pad lift and carpet behavior against your actual rugs.

IF YOU HAVE CORDS

Obstacle avoidance lowers risk; it doesn’t remove it.

Even advanced sensors can miss thin cables, flat objects, dark surfaces, and pet accidents. A little floor pickup still helps.

IF YOU WANT LESS WORK

Every “hands-free” dock still has a routine.

Dust bags, dirty-water tanks, mop trays, and filters need attention. Choose which chores you want the dock to take over.

THE DAY-TO-DAY DECISION

Who each pick suits — and when to spend more.

These notes combine published product details with attributed outside testing. They are not DustMigo hands-on reviews. Use the finder for a quick match, then check the model notes that fit your home.

LOWER SPEND · VACUUM-FIRST

Roborock Q10 S5+

Choose it if
You want mapped daily vacuuming and an auto-empty bag at the lower reference tier, and you are comfortable washing the single mop pad yourself after mopping.
Skip it if
Cords, socks, or toys often stay on the floor, or you mop often and do not want to handle a damp pad and water tank. Its obstacle sensor is a weak point in published testing.
Routine to expect
Wash the mop pad by hand after a mop run; check the main and side brushes for hair, clean the filter as directed, and replace the dock bag when full.
When to spend more
Move to the Qrevo S5V if automatic mop washing and drying would save you regular work. If you mostly vacuum open rooms and can pick up loose items, the higher tier may add little for your routine.
What outside tests support

Vacuum Wars says it bought and tested the exact model. Its review reports 96% pickup in its carpet deep-clean test and 96% flattened pet-hair pickup, but only 6 of 24 obstacles avoided (its test average: 16.6). It also flags early customer quality-control reports as a signal to monitor, not a long-term reliability result. Read Vacuum Wars’ test (updated Sep 1, 2026).

MIDRANGE · AUTOMATIC MOP CARE

Roborock Qrevo S5V

Choose it if
You mop regularly on mixed floors and want the dock to empty dust, wash and warm-air dry the pads, and refill the robot. The 10 mm mop lift is aimed at short-pile rugs.
Skip it if
Your rugs have a tall pile, or loose cables and small objects are a routine hazard. A 10 mm lift does not guarantee dry high-pile rugs, and its tested obstacle avoidance was poor.
Routine to expect
Refill clean water and empty dirty water when prompted; clean the dock’s wash area periodically, replace the dust bag, and inspect the brush for hair. The dock reduces mop handling; it does not remove dock maintenance.
When to spend more
Pay for the Saros 10R only if very low furniture or clutter makes its stronger obstacle sensing useful; consider the Dreame if its carpet and edge-cleaning features fit your rooms. If your rugs are short and you mop often, this tier may be enough.
What outside tests support

Vacuum Wars says it bought the exact model and ran its performance test suite. It reports 92% medium-pile carpet debris removal and 92% flattened pet-hair pickup, alongside only 6 of 24 obstacles avoided. Its tested dock washes and warm-air dries pads but does not wash them with heated water. Read Vacuum Wars’ test (updated Sep 18, 2026).

MIDRANGE · PETS AND CARPET

Dreame L40 Ultra Gen 2

Choose it if
You have shedding pets, a mix of carpet and hard floors, and expect to use the extendable cleaning features and automatic mop-care dock. Its 25,000 Pa figure is a manufacturer claim, not a DustMigo measurement.
Skip it if
You mainly vacuum open hard floors and would rarely use mop care or the extra reach; a simpler tier may cover that routine. Also check the box contents: TriCut and the automatic solution dispenser are not in every standard bundle.
Routine to expect
Fill the clean-water tank and empty the dirty-water tank, clean the dock wash area and filters as directed, and remove hair from the brush when needed. The dock automates pad care, not water handling or periodic cleaning.
When to spend more
Move up to the Saros 10R if low clearance or clutter is the deciding problem. Otherwise the premium tier may add little unless its extra navigation features solve a specific issue in your home.
What outside testing supports

Consumer Reports lists the exact L40 Ultra Gen 2 in its robot vacuum-and-mop lab test program. The public page confirms lab evaluation but hides the scores and verdict behind membership; DustMigo therefore does not infer a performance result from it. See Consumer Reports’ model page.

PREMIUM · LOW CLEARANCE AND CLUTTER

Roborock Saros 10R

Choose it if
Furniture clearance near 3.14 in rules out taller robots, or you often need better recognition around floor clutter. Measure the narrowest point; the listed robot height is not a guarantee it will pass under every piece of furniture.
Skip it if
Your rooms are open and furniture has ample clearance: the premium tier may buy features you will not use. Even its sensors can miss a shoelace lying flat, so keep thin cords clear.
Routine to expect
Like other full-service docks, it still needs clean water, dirty-water disposal, tray cleaning, a dust bag, and brush/filter care. Mop washing and automatic mop-mount removal reduce specific chores rather than eliminating upkeep.
When the premium makes sense
Spend more when low furniture or frequent clutter is a real daily obstacle. For ordinary-height rooms where mop washing is the main convenience you want, compare the Qrevo S5V first.
What an independent reviewer found

Notebookcheck’s Marcus Schwarten tested a manufacturer-supplied unit; the publication says the maker did not influence or preview the review. It found strong obstacle detection—including cables and socks—but missed a flat shoelace, and noted some navigation and dried-stain mopping limits. Read the Saros 10R test (updated Feb 18, 2026).
